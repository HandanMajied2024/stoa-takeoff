# Measure geometry, let the agent only explain

The winning design for this hackathon is a **hybrid pipeline in which code measures and counts, and the LLM only plans, queries and explains**. Every independent benchmark in the notes shows that frontier vision-language models (VLMs) still cannot be trusted with exact counts or 2% areas from pixels. They count doors and windows correctly only **34–55% of the time** on AECV-Bench, and they estimate room areas with **about 43% MAPE**. A plain pixel or vector measurement reaches **1.6–5.7% MAPE** on the same task. Hybrid detector-plus-agent systems are the only ones that beat human estimators on a takeoff benchmark (81.6 against 77.6). Your own results fit this pattern: 296/300 Swiss areas and 150/150 IFC areas within 2% came from computed geometry, and the stored IFC areas were wrong. The benchmark's real battleground is therefore the **85,514 CubiCasa count questions (67.3% of 127,044)**. They form roughly 43k unique questions, because each one is asked in English and again in Finnish, over 4,787 raster plans. For those, the best available tool is a YOLO detector fine-tuned on CubiCasa's own SVG annotations. Those annotations carry showers, sauna stoves and stairs that the official CubiCasa model never learned. Commercially, detection is already a $900–$1,800 per user per year add-on at US incumbents, and none of them speaks Finnish or Talo 2000. Stoa's differentiation therefore lies downstream of detection: cited, confidence-scored, revision-aware quantities coded to Nordic standards and linked to carbon and ordering.

## Benchmark scores leave counting and area to deterministic code

The research pins down where VLMs fail, and it matches the scoring rule of `questions.jsonl` almost exactly. On **AECV-Bench** (120 plans, partly CubiCasa5k), mean counting accuracy was **51% for Gemini 3 Pro, 49% for GPT-5.2 and 42% for Claude Opus 4.5**. Per class, doors scored only **39%**, windows **34%** and toilets 82%, against 91% for bedrooms ([AECFoundry](https://www.aecfoundry.com/blog/can-ai-really-read-your-building-plans-aecv-bench-gets-a-major-upgrade); [arXiv 2601.04819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04819)). The same authors report that OCR-style questions reach about 0.95. Reading labels like "MH" or "12,5 m²" is therefore not the bottleneck. Symbol counting is. On structural drawings, quantity takeoff was the weakest category for nearly every model in **DrawingVQA**, where the best QTO score was 41.7 ([arXiv 2607.15418](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15418)). Togal's vendor-run "Construction's Last Exam" put general LLMs without tools at 2–34% ([Deadfront](https://www.deadfront.ai/blog/november-2026-ai-construction-roundup)). The one optimistic data point is COXIT's November 2026 **CaseVBench**, where GPT-6 Astra reached 92% and Claude Opus 5.5 79% on IoU-matched detection on page crops ([COXIT](https://coxit.co/ai-drawing-benchmark/)). It is vendor-run, though, and scores boxes on crops rather than exact whole-plan totals. Treat it as evidence of fast progress, not as permission to count with a VLM.

Areas tell the same story more sharply. A 2026 study found open VLMs estimating room areas at **R² ≈ 0.71 and MAPE ≈ 43%**, worst on small rooms. A colour-segmentation pixel count on the same plans reached **5.68% per room and 1.58% per plan** ([Periodica Polytechnica](https://pp.bme.hu/ci/article/view/44138)). A 2% tolerance rules out any estimate that is not computed from geometry.

The failure modes explain the gap and point to the fixes. AECV-Bench lists these errors: door swings confused with other arcs, doors without arcs missed, glazed doors counted as windows, fixtures hallucinated in dense areas, and **double doors counted inconsistently (per opening vs per leaf)** ([AECFoundry](https://www.aecfoundry.com/blog/can-ai-really-read-your-building-plans-aecv-bench-gets-a-major-upgrade)). That last error matters directly here. The benchmark's door question spells out a convention ("count doorways fitted with a door; open doorways without a door leaf do not count"), and your counting logic must encode it explicitly. Resolution is the second fix. Claude 4.7+ silently downscales anything above a **2,576 px long edge** ([Claude vision docs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision)). Fixed overlapping crops alone raised LED-BENCH accuracy from **36.4 to 49.3**, about the same gain as a three-call search-then-reread loop ([arXiv 2610.19571](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.19571)). The architectures that win are hybrids. **Handoff-H1** pairs CV models with tool-using agents and scored **81.6**, against 77.6 for professional estimators and 35–61 for frontier models ([arXiv 2608.15032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15032)). On IFC, agents that iteratively query the model reach about 80% on IFC-Bench, and the authors find that **BIM model quality matters more than the LLM backbone** ([EC3 2025](https://ec-3.org/publication/ec32025_265/); [arXiv 2605.01698](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01698)). That is exactly the lesson your wrong IFC pset areas taught.

## CubiCasa counts reward a YOLO model trained on SVG labels

CubiCasa5k is mostly Finnish (5,000 plans drawn from about 15,000 Finnish images). Its polygon annotations cover more than 80 categories ([arXiv 1904.01920](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.01920)). The official multi-task model is a weak base for exact counting. Its raw icon mIoU is **55.7%**, falling to 41.6% after polygonization, and bathtub IoU is only **26.7%**. It also runs on PyTorch 1.0 in a repository with only 13 commits ([paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.01920); [GitHub](https://github.com/CubiCasa/CubiCasa5k)). The most useful finding sits in its loader. The SVGs define **52 icon types and 65 room types**, including Shower, ShowerCab, SaunaBench, SaunaStove, WashingMachine and Stairs. The training remap, however, **drops Shower entirely** and merges Sauna into Bath, and the Stairs parser is commented out ([house.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CubiCasa/CubiCasa5k/master/floortrans/loaders/house.py)). The benchmark asks precisely about showers plus bathtubs, saunas, sinks (with a double-sink rule), WC seats, kitchens, bedrooms, bathrooms, balconies and storeys. Only a custom parse of the SVGs gives labels for all of them.

The practical route is to convert `FixedFurniture`, `Door` and `Window` polygons from the Zenodo SVGs (5.5 GB, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) into YOLO boxes, then fine-tune YOLOv8 or YOLO11 ([Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/2613548)). Room-type counts (MH, KPH, SAUNA, WC, PARVEKE) then come from OCR of the room labels plus the `Space` polygons, not from detection. For a day-one zero-shot baseline, **SamirShabani/Architect** is a YOLOv8m model trained on FloorPlanCAD renders with 28 classes, among them door, window, toilet, sink, bathtub and stairs. It reports mAP50 of 0.877 and is CC BY-NC. Its own model card warns that it degrades on noisy scans ([HF](https://huggingface.co/SamirShabani/Architect/blob/main/README.md)). Two research successors are worth a look if time allows. **fpvec-lab** publishes *corrected* CubiCasa annotations and a wall/opening benchmark; reading its opening heatmap raised opening F1 2.6x ([arXiv 2608.25608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25608)). **Raster2Seq** (SIGGRAPH 2026) claims state of the art on CubiCasa for polygon output, but its code release is unconfirmed ([arXiv 2602.09016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.09016)).

There is an integrity trap that the notes do not address but the dataset makes obvious. The 4,787 benchmark PNGs are CubiCasa plans, and their answers almost certainly derive from the same public SVGs. Training on, or looking up, the annotation of the very plan being asked about is leakage. Judges would rightly discount it, and it would not generalize to a customer's scan. Keep CubiCasa's official 4,200/400/400 split, report accuracy on held-out plans, and say so on stage. A fair score that includes the Finnish sauna classes is a stronger story than an inflated one.

For OCR, **PP-OCRv5** (Apache-2.0, 5M parameters) reports 0.067 on OmniDocBench against 0.090 for Surya, plus strong 90°-rotation results. Surya is GPL-3 ([arXiv 2603.24373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24373); [Roboflow](https://playground.roboflow.com/models/compare/doctr-vs-surya)). The accuracy gain comes from post-processing more than from engine choice. Normalise `,`/`.` decimals and `m2`/`m²`, then match tokens against a closed Finnish vocabulary (MH, OH, K, KPH, PH, S, WC, KHH, ET, VH, PARVEKE) and assign each label to the room polygon containing it. No dataset or model targets hand-drawn or historic scans; searches found only old Hough and subgraph-isomorphism work ([DFKI](https://www-live.dfki.de/fileadmin/user_upload/import/5675_4520a864.pdf)). The finna, HABS and Europeana archive drawings will therefore depend on deskewing, binarization and optional super-resolution ([rbg-research](https://github.com/rbg-research/Floor-Plan-Detection)), with an honest "low confidence" flag rather than a fabricated number. None of those sources carries benchmark questions anyway, so they are demo material, not score material.

## Vector and IFC inputs already solve the 2% rule, if you compute

For Swiss DXF/PDF and the IFC models, which hold the remaining 33% of questions including all 14,400+ area, length and volume questions, the tooling is mature and permissive. **ezdxf** (MIT) handles layer and block queries, so a door count can be as simple as `msp.query('INSERT[name=="DOOR_*"]')`. You must still handle nested and dynamic blocks ([ezdxf](https://ezdxf.readthedocs.io/en/stable/introduction.html); [blocks tutorial](https://sources.debian.org/src/ezdxf/1.4.1-1/docs/source/tutorials/blocks.rst)). DWG needs a converter: LibreDWG's `dwg2dxf` is GPL-3 and should be isolated as a CLI ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LibreDWG)), while the ODA File Converter is proprietary freeware with unverified server terms. **PyMuPDF's `get_drawings()`** is the best vector-PDF extractor, but it is AGPL. It also returns clipped and hidden paths, which inflate counts on CAD-exported PDFs ([PyMuPDF docs](https://pymupdf.readthedocs.io/en/latest/recipes-drawing-and-graphics.html)). It is fine for a hackathon. For a product, either buy the Artifex licence or use pdfplumber or pypdfium2. No open-source scale or title-block detector exists, so scale comes from regex on `1:50` plus a sanity check against a known door width.

**IfcOpenShell 0.8.x** (LGPL) is the IFC core ([PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/ifcopenshell/0.8.3/)). Its QTO path is still being fixed. IfcSpace GrossFloorArea was only recently wired into ifc5d, and `get_volume` now returns NaN on non-watertight meshes instead of a wrong value ([commit](https://dev.glassvision.dedyn.io/Glassvision/IfcOpenShell/src/commit/de40b5f685379bf93de8071f7d9a811522db05c9)). Stored `Qto_*` psets reflect whatever the authoring tool exported, so the right pattern is **compute, compare with the stored value, and flag the difference**. Your 150/150 result shows why. That discrepancy should be a visible product feature, not a hidden fix. For the 820 IFC-Bench text questions, the evidence favours giving the agent a sandboxed IfcOpenShell tool plus a precomputed quantity table over static query generation ([arXiv 2605.01698](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.01698)). The pip-installable **`ifcmcp`** in the IfcOpenShell repo is the most deployable MCP server ([repo](https://dev.glassvision.dedyn.io/Glassvision/IfcOpenShell/src/tag/bonsai-0.8.6-alpha2608231326/src/ifcmcp)). An open project, **ifc-agent-tools**, already wraps deterministic QTO as a Claude skill ([GitHub](https://github.com/masoodkaramoozian/ifc-agent-tools)).

For viewing, IfcConvert exports GLB, and **@thatopen/fragments 3.x is MIT** ([docs](https://docs.thatopen.com/fragments/getting-started)). Raster and vector plans need no IFC at all: extruding the room and wall polygons with three.js `ExtrudeGeometry` gives an instant 3D view that runs on phones. Generating IFC from detected rooms with `ifcopenshell.api` is easy. The hard parts are upstream (wall centerlines, closing rooms across door gaps, storey heights), and no mature open vector-PDF-to-IFC tool exists ([OSArch](https://community.osarch.org/discussion/comment/20546/)). Avoid xeokit (AGPL) and turf.js area functions, which assume WGS84 coordinates and are wrong for planar drawings.

## Competitors sell detection; nobody sells trust in Finnish

The market has split three ways. **Togal** ($299/user/month, about $22.65M raised), **STACK** (FloorPlan AI as an $899 add-on) and **Kreo** sell self-serve AI takeoff. **Beam/Attentive** ($30.5M Series B, Nov 2025) sells an AI-plus-human service at $15–25K per trade per year. **Bluebeam Max** and Autodesk Forma bolt AI onto incumbent tools ([ConstructConnect](https://www.constructconnect.com/blog/ai-powered-takeoff-and-estimating-software-a-contractors-guide-to-the-top-players-in-2026); [FinSMEs](https://www.finsmes.com/2025/11/attentive-ai-raises-30-5m-in-series-b-funding.html); [Architosh](https://architosh.com/2026/05/ai-powered-bluebeam-max-launches-globally/)). Togal.CHAT and STACK Plan Chat already pair chat with takeoff, so "chat with your plans" is not novel on its own. The money is moving to the document and review layer: **Trunk Tools ($70M)**, **Buildcheck ($18M)**, whose expert-confirmed "Custom Checks" turn QA manuals into agents, and **Document Crunch**, now part of Trimble and reading drawings with source-linked citations since October 2026 ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2025/07/24/trunk-tools-raises-40m-revolutionize-construction-teams-interact-project-data/); [AEC Magazine](https://aecmag.com/construction/buildcheck-raises-12m-in-series-a/); [Deadfront](https://www.deadfront.ai/blog/november-2026-ai-construction-roundup)). In the Nordics, **Dalux** shipped a document AI assistant in 2026.1 and is Stoa's most direct threat ([Dalux](https://support.dalux.com/hc/en-us/articles/23976440268572)). Funding there goes to estimating and design (Brickanta $8M, Endra $50M) and to 2D-to-BIM for renovation (**Make a BIM**, Finnish, pre-seed) ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/01/27/brickanta-closes-8m-round-to-expand-ai-use-in-construction-planning/); [Kiilto](https://www.kiilto.com/newsroom/finnish-proptech-startup-make-a-bim-secures-funding-to-accelerate-digitisation-of-aging-building-stock/)). No Finnish AI takeoff product turned up. CubiCasa itself, though Finnish-born, now serves US real estate under Clear Capital ([Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/cubicasa)).

User pain is consistent and points to trust, not detection. In one study cited by vendors, an AI takeoff took 1 minute and manual correction took about 30 ([ConstructConnect](https://www.constructconnect.com/blog/are-ai-takeoffs-accurate-in-2026)). Accuracy collapses on scans, hand-marked revisions and wrong scales, producing takeoffs that "look clean" but are wrong ([Technbrains](https://www.technbrains.com/blog/ai-construction-estimating/)). Kreo users report that reorganising messy auto-measures eats the time saved ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/231342/Kreo-2D-Takeoff/)). The local hooks are concrete. **Talo 2000** sets national quantity-calculation rules ([Rakennustieto](https://www.rakennustieto.fi/en/talo-2000-nimikkeistot)). YTV requires quantities **by location** ([Tampere thesis](https://trepo.tuni.fi/bitstream/123456789/21530/3/uusitalo.pdf)). Decree 2/2026 imposed carbon-footprint limits on new buildings from 9 January 2026, backed by the free **CO2data.fi** database ([Syke](https://www.syke.fi/en/environmental-data/calculators-and-tools/co2data-service/emissions-database-building-construction)).

## Recommended 2–3 day hackathon stack

| Layer | Choice | Why / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Canonical model | One JSON schema: `Element{id, kind, class, polygon, area_m2, source:{file, page, bbox/entity_id, method}, confidence}` | Every input format lands here. Every answer cites element ids. This is the traceability backbone. |
| Raster (CubiCasa, scans) | YOLO11/YOLOv8 fine-tuned on CubiCasa SVG-derived boxes (train split only), with tiled inference + NMS; SamirShabani/Architect as a day-1 baseline | Covers Shower/SaunaStove/Stairs, which the official model lacks. Report held-out accuracy. |
| Raster text | PP-OCRv5 with orientation classification, then Finnish abbreviation regex mapped to room polygons | Room-type counts (MH, KPH, WC, S, PARVEKE) from labels, not pixels. |
| Raster rooms | OpenCV binarize/deskew + wall mask + flood fill/`shapely.polygonize`; CubiCasa model as a wall prior if it runs | Polygons for the viewer. Areas only when a scale is known. |
| Vector DXF/DWG | ezdxf → shapely (LibreDWG `dwg2dxf` as a separate CLI for DWG) | Your proven 296/300 path. |
| Vector PDF/SVG | PyMuPDF `get_drawings()` (hackathon; swap to pypdfium2/pdfplumber for production) + svgpathtools | Apply clipping. Regex the scale. |
| IFC | IfcOpenShell 0.8.x: compute areas from geometry, diff against stored psets; `ifcmcp` or a sandboxed Python tool for IFC-Bench text questions | Your proven 150/150 path. Show the pset discrepancy. |
| Agent | Claude with tools: `query_elements`, `count(class, filter)`, `area(ids)`, `crop_image(bbox)`, `ifc_python`; the VLM only verifies crops and is never the source of a number | Expandable reasoning = tool-call trace + cited element ids + crop thumbnails. |
| Eval harness | Script over `questions.jsonl` that scores exact counts and 2% areas per source and question class, plus a confusion table | Drives the demo slide and catches regressions. |
| 2D viewer | SVG/canvas overlay of polygons on the original raster or pdf.js page, with click-to-highlight from chat citations | The trust feature. |
| 3D viewer | three.js / React Three Fiber `ExtrudeGeometry` from room and wall polygons; GLB via IfcConvert or @thatopen/fragments for IFC | Light enough for mobile. |
| Mobile | Responsive PWA: camera upload of a paper plan → same pipeline | Shows the on-site story. |

Batch API pricing makes full-benchmark VLM verification affordable if needed. At about $0.04 per page for Claude Opus 5.5, with a 50% batch discount, 4,787 plans cost on the order of $100 ([COXIT](https://coxit.co/ai-drawing-benchmark/); [Anthropic batch docs](https://platform.claude.com/en/docs/build-with-claude/batch-processing)). Use it as a second opinion on disagreements, not as the primary counter.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the hackathon. The question is not which VLM reads drawings best but **how little the VLM should be trusted with**, and the answer is: nothing that produces a number. Your vector and IFC results already show that the remaining risk sits almost entirely in raster symbol counting on CubiCasa. That makes CubiCasa's under-exploited SVG vocabulary (showers, sauna stoves, stairs), together with a strict held-out split and an explicit door-leaf convention, the highest-leverage two days of work. A second, less obvious takeaway is that the IFC pset error you found is the product's thesis in miniature. Source data lies quietly, and a tool that measures, compares and shows its evidence is worth more than one that answers fluently.

Several points remain uncertain. No independent re-run of AECV-Bench on November 2026 frontier models exists, so VLM crop verification may now be stronger than the late-2025 numbers suggest. Fine-tuned VLMs for floor-plan *counting* are essentially unstudied. The licence terms of CubiCasa data, PyMuPDF and the ODA converter all mean the hackathon stack is not the production stack. Strategically, cited Q&A is becoming table stakes inside Dalux, Bluebeam and Autodesk. Stoa's defensible ground is the intersection that none of them occupies: Finnish-language, Talo 2000-coded, carbon-aware quantities that a site manager can verify on a phone in one tap.
